## Genuine observed weakness W1 — capacity dependency and weak operational timing
Small recommendation_management_implication:
"Top action 1 ... executive sponsor with legal/security and quality functions produces a signed pilot charter, permission matrix and release/escalation/stop decision rights"
"Top action 2 ... editorial/quality lead with research and IT/data leads produces a segregated sandbox procedure ... competency exercise and access test"
"Top action 3 ... finance with sponsor and proposed service owner records pilot staff time, reviewer coverage, fallback, feedback and support limits"

O2/O7 provide minimal training time, O3 leaves protected review capacity undocumented, O4 limits IT support, and O5 supplies no committed pilot allocation. Yet the recommendation labels action 1 "dependency first" and only commits delivery capacity in action 3. This leaves unclear who can perform the earlier work, whether actions 1/2 are preparation or a staffed pilot, and what must happen if minimum oversight is unavailable. This is a sequencing/decision-usefulness weakness, not evidence that the permission controls are wrong or that unsafe operation actually occurred. The small response DOES identify capacity risk elsewhere; the defect is that it does not operationalize that risk as an early dependency in the ranked recommendation.

All three recommendation_management_implication fields end with approximately-three-month reassessment language: primary "Reassess evidence within the approximate three-month management window"; mature "Within approximately three months reassess gate evidence, workload and errors"; small "Within approximately three months, reassess based on records, not a promised completion date." Sensible caution avoids an invented completion promise, but provides no concrete intervening decision sequence or relative earliest permissible start based on each profile. The mature case could verify and reuse controls while the constrained case first establishes minimum capacity; the recommendation text leaves those paths only partly explicit.

Root cause hypothesis: v0.1 required three ranked actions and stage gates but did not require a minimum-capacity dependency before the ranked implementation work, an explicit start/hold decision for a staffed pilot, or intermediate event-based checkpoints inside the decision window. This is an instruction-design inference, not a proven causal claim about model internals.

Impact: management must infer sequence from distributed detail and may treat three broadly similar gate lists as equally executable. No fabricated numerical dates or differing verdict labels are needed to correct it.
