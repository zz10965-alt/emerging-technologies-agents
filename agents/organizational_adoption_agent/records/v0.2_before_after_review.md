Read alone, it could imply that a full client-permission inquiry blocks a public/synthetic test unnecessarily. The same output explicitly allows narrower sandbox work without enterprise integration, distinguishes real-client permission gates, and permits covered permission work in parallel. This is residual sequencing ambiguity, not an observed unsafe authorization or a reason to pretend the improvement failed. Interpret entry gates against the precise proposed exposure and obtain management clarification before operational use.

Mature v0.2 is more conservative about sandbox entry than mature v0.1: it requires committed staffing/internal test authorization rather than suggesting work is already permitted under controls. On this case's unverified time allocations that is defensible; it does not erase documented strengths or prove the firm is slower. "Relatively high" confidence in internal reasoning in mature v0.2 remains separate from explicitly low confidence in actual execution readiness; it should not become a team confidence score.
