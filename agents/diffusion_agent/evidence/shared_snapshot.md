## What has changed since v0.1
The original product documents remain as access/ecosystem evidence D1–D3. E1–E4 add actual survey findings, question wording, historical coding-task use, methods and qualitative use practices. All empirical additions share DORA/Google Cloud as issuer; E2 reuses E4. Do not count them as independent replications. Direct original healthcare, METR and Stack Overflow routes were blocked; no uninspected contents or AI-generated summaries from those sources are admitted.

## Adoption-measure ladder — keep these units separate
1. Awareness: having heard of a tool; no representative awareness measure admitted here.
2. Availability/access and experimentation: product access/free entry or a trial is not adoption. D1–D3 support technical access descriptions only; no experiment-to-use conversion rate is supplied.
3. Individual current use: E1 reports current workplace AI use among surveyed technology professionals. The question does not isolate only LLMs or only code assistants.
4. Individual recurring use: E1 supplies a retrospective three-month task-frequency item; E4 supplies historical task-reliance findings. This is self-reported frequency/reliance, not measured daily telemetry, cohort retention or institutional deployment.
5. Sustained organizational deployment: requires a defined organization/team denominator, authorized production scope, repeated active-use observations, duration, workflow integration and retention; NOT established by this dossier. Seat purchases, individual use and strategy priorities do not establish it.
6. Industry-level penetration: requires comparable institutions, geography, date and routine-use definition. Technical-worker survey percentages do not measure software-company or hospital penetration. A technical respondent employed in healthcare is not evidence that clinicians use ambient documentation.

The ladder is a measurement distinction, not a claim that every organization passes through fixed linear stages. There is no verified conversion rate between its levels.

## Interpreting the empirical evidence without overclaiming
E1 supports substantial reported workplace use and recurring task use among the surveyed technical workers. E4 supports historical coding-task assistance in its respondent subset. These are evidence of use beyond mere product availability, but neither proves all LLMs have reached a global Rogers stage. Software-task evidence may inform the coding application while remaining a sector-specific, broad-AI proxy for general LLM diffusion. In the hospital case the same general/sector evidence stays the same; it does not transfer to clinical documentation. Posture changes organizational timing, not these findings.

Do not calculate a longitudinal growth rate by subtracting E4's 75.9% from E1's 90%: samples, wording, denominators, collection periods and scope may differ, and no common cohort was verified. E2's rounded 76% and E4's 75.9% are the same underlying 2024 finding, not two independent observations. E1's 60% is conditional on surveyed AI users; it is not 60% of all professionals, all firms or daily users. Three-month recall is not longitudinal persistence. AI tools include a broader class than the target LLM-based technology; no LLM-only penetration percentage is established.

Exact current global Rogers stage, global prevalence, local peer rank, sustained enterprise rollout, adoption-to-productivity causation and industry saturation are unsupported. A qualitative stage hypothesis may be discussed for a named surveyed social system, with uncertainty; do not force a category or convert Rogers' theoretical proportions into measured thresholds. The most defensible current finding can acknowledge substantial surveyed technical-worker use while leaving exact 2026 population/industry stages unresolved.

E1 and E4 adoption observations are older than the proposed twelve-month refresh flag as of November 2026. E3's 2026 publication does not update their prevalence estimates. Its student findings support bounded mechanism hypotheses about trust, training, verification and skill concerns, not company or hospital adoption rates. E2's model associations must not be rewritten as randomized causal effects or guaranteed accelerator percentages. No strategy-effect percentage is admitted to this snapshot.

## A required meaningful weak claim — retained, not repaired by inference
“Most comparable hospitals now routinely use LLM-assisted clinical documentation” remains unsupported. There is no inspected original representative hospital-level documentation survey, routine-use definition, specialty coverage, geographic frame, denominator or sustained deployment measure. D3 concerns a medical foundation-model ecosystem, not ambient-scribe adoption. E1/E4 concern technical roles, not hospital documentation users. Abstain from this penetration/peer-stage claim; request a dated original hospital-level survey or administrative dataset with the missing attributes. A small local clinical outcome study, even if later supplied, would not alone measure industry penetration.

Also qualify “most comparable software firms have sustained company-wide deployment of LLM coding assistants.” Individual technical-worker current/recurrent use and respondent-reported team incorporation do not establish a firm-level authorized deployment denominator. Request repeated organization/team telemetry with meaningful active-use, workflow scope, governance and retention definitions.

## Management measurement implications — proposals, not collected facts
For coding cases, candidate evaluation owners should distinguish eligibility, access, trial, meaningful weekly active use, repeated cohort retention, team workflow integration and quality-adjusted outcomes. For hospital documentation, accountable clinical/informatics/governance owners should first establish exact documentation scope, jurisdiction, EHR/data-handling prerequisites, clinician authority and comparable evidence. Review gates and windows are proposals for local management; they are not observed deployment or adoption authorization.
