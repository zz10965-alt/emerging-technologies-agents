## Reproducible eight-dimension framework
For EACH numbered dimension in organization_specific_finding include these compact labeled elements: Strength; Gap/failure mechanism; Evidence sufficiency/missing input; Business consequence; Priority/reason; Corrective action and gate; Proposed accountable function; Reassessment trigger. Cite O identifiers in each row. If strength or gap is not evidenced, say not established; do not manufacture balance. Check that the eight rows do not merely repeat one general checklist.
1. Leadership / executive sponsorship: distinguish enthusiasm from a sponsor's decision rights and escalation mandate. Suggested function: executive sponsor.
2. Workforce capabilities / skills: distinguish market expertise from source checking, safe-data handling and AI-use skills. Suggested function: research lead with training lead.
3. Workflow / process integration: locate source traceability, expert review, release authority and fallback in the actual manual/report process. Suggested function: editorial/quality lead.
4. Infrastructure / data readiness: identify approved data inventory, access permissions, permitted processing environment, integration and access boundaries; do not equate fragmented data with unusable technology. Suggested function: IT/data lead with information security.
5. Resources / budget: separate contemplated pilot spending, committed pilot resources and continuing support capacity; do not invent amounts or financial returns. Suggested function: finance with sponsor.
6. Governance / risk / accountability: identify client-information rules, review authority, permitted data, escalation and stop decisions. Suggested function: sponsor with legal/security and quality leads; do not assert applicable law or contractual permission.
7. Culture / organizational change: use receptiveness and training evidence to choose supported engagement and feedback measures. Suggested function: change/research lead.
8. Operating ownership / long-term sustainability: distinguish named service responsibility, incident coverage, maintenance, monitoring and funded continuing operation. Suggested function: sponsor appoints service owner; absence of documentation is not proof nobody owns it.

## Priority and decision procedure
Use qualitative states per dimension: supported for the specified stage / conditional on evidence or remediation / not assessable. No aggregate score. Use consequence, dependency and immediacy to rank only the consequential gaps:
- P0: prerequisite before the specific exposure (e.g., permission before any real client information, accountable release/stop authority before external delivery).
- P1: needed to operate and evaluate the bounded pilot effectively.
- P2: prerequisite to broader or sustained deployment; escalate to P0/P1 only if the pilot itself depends on it.
Explain why the same issue may have different priority for different scopes. Do not make complete enterprise integration a prerequisite for a segregated synthetic/public-data sandbox. Conversely, anonymization or public inputs alone do not authorize external client delivery or establish safe processing.
Choose and justify one bounded recommendation: proceed to controlled pilot; proceed only after defined gates; or defer the proposed exposure. Distinguish (a) internal synthetic/public-data sandbox, no client delivery, (b) controlled workflow involving actual client data or client-facing use, and (c) scale/ongoing production. These are gating distinctions within the same application, not different applications for the contrast test. State what may begin now, what is conditional, what cannot yet be recommended, and what to reassess within the approximate three-month decision window. Do not promise completion in three months.
Rank the top three actions with dependency order, explicit O evidence, concrete deliverable, proposed owner and acceptance evidence. Do not mechanically rank every dimension equally. Preserve organizational advantages and explain how they reduce a specific remediation burden. For more mature profiles, do not prescribe creating controls that the case already supplies; verify their scope/effectiveness instead. For constrained profiles, consider narrower internal scope, manual controls, reduced integration and deferral if minimum oversight cannot be resourced; outsourcing is not proof of permission or accountability.

## Context-sensitive execution sequence (v0.2 targeted control)
A shared headline recommendation can be justified by shared permission/quality unknowns. Do not force different verdicts, invent faster dates for larger firms, or soften common client-information and release gates merely to make contexts differ. Show how ORGANIZATIONAL EVIDENCE changes the work required, dependency order and earliest permissible activity.

In recommendation_management_implication, before the ranked implementation actions, state a minimum viable accountable capacity decision: is there evidenced sponsor/decision coverage, allocated operator/reviewer time, minimum support and an authorized resource envelope for the proposed bounded exposure? Distinguish approval of a budget or existence of a role from proven sufficiency. If capacity is unknown, assign a proposed functional owner to verify/commit it before any staffed implementation/evaluation that depends on it; do not bury this dependency after the work it enables. A brief capacity/authority check can precede or accompany planning without requiring a full enterprise operating model. Permission review and capacity verification may run in parallel if named coverage for both is established. If minimum oversight cannot be committed, defer staffed testing and allow only explicitly authorized preparation that does not assume that capacity.

Then provide these labeled STRING subsections within recommendation_management_implication:
- Now: distinguish non-operational preparation from staffed internal sandbox testing; say start / conditional / defer for the latter, with O evidence and remaining prerequisites. This is advice, not the agent granting authorization.
- Next: top three actions in dependency order with proposed owner, concrete acceptance evidence and dependencies; say create / verify / reuse / supplement according to the profile. Missing capacity is an early prerequisite, even where permission remains the hard gate before client data.
- Decision checkpoints within the approximate three-month window: propose an initial capacity-and-scope decision, an evaluation-entry decision after prerequisites, and a continue/hold/stop review based on recorded quality, review burden and support. - Client exposure and scale: state unchanged exposure-specific permission/processing/release gates and separate ongoing ownership/funding/effectiveness conditions. Internal testing alone never licenses external delivery.

A mature profile should reuse documented sponsorship, identified owner, trained cohort, approved pilot budget and editorial/access rules while verifying scope, practical capacity and effectiveness. A mixed profile may need to establish missing authority/workflow arrangements and commit resources before testing. A constrained profile must resolve minimal reviewer/operator/support capacity before assuming implementation work is feasible; narrow scope or defer if that prerequisite cannot be met. These are evidence-sensitive paths, not predetermined verdicts.

For all eight dimensions, count as a strength only an evidenced enabling capability. A limited budget, missing owner or awareness that evidence is absent is not itself a readiness strength. When none is supported, say "No additional strength established" and retain genuine assets elsewhere. Proposed caps, clarification and controls remain proposals or explicit inferences, not supplied organizational facts.
